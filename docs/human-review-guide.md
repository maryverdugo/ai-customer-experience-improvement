# Human Review Guide

## Purpose

The AI Customer Experience Improvement System is designed to assist people, not replace human judgment.

AI can organize feedback, identify possible patterns, suggest possible root causes, and surface improvement opportunities. A human reviewer must verify important findings and decide what action, if any, should be taken.

> **Core principle:** AI identifies possibilities. People verify reality and make the final decisions.

---

## 1. What the AI Can Do

The AI may help with:

- Organizing customer feedback
- Categorizing issues
- Summarizing individual comments
- Identifying possible recurring themes
- Connecting related feedback
- Suggesting possible root causes
- Describing potential customer impact
- Identifying possible business or process impact
- Suggesting improvement options
- Identifying questions that require additional investigation

These outputs are analysis and suggestions, not automatically verified facts.

---

## 2. What the Human Must Verify

Before acting on an AI-generated finding, the reviewer should verify:

### The Evidence

- Does the original customer feedback support the finding?
- Are we accurately representing what the customer said?
- Are multiple examples actually describing the same issue?
- Is the sample large enough to support a broader conclusion?

### The Pattern

- Is this a recurring pattern or an isolated incident?
- How frequently does the issue occur in the real customer population?
- Could the apparent pattern be caused by how the feedback was collected?

### The Root Cause

- Is the proposed root cause supported by operational evidence?
- Are there other plausible explanations?
- What data would confirm or challenge the hypothesis?

### The Impact

- Is customer impact documented or only inferred?
- Is business impact measured or only possible?
- Which customers or processes are actually affected?

### The Recommendation

- Is the proposed improvement appropriate?
- What could the unintended consequences be?
- What resources would be required?
- Can the idea be tested before a larger rollout?

---

## 3. Evidence Levels

Use clear language to distinguish evidence from interpretation.

### Level 1 — Customer Report

Something the customer directly reported.

**Example:**

> "I called twice about the same issue."

This is evidence of what the customer reported.

### Level 2 — Observed Pattern

Multiple feedback records contain similar information.

**Example:**

> Three fictional feedback records describe repeated contact.

This supports further investigation but does not establish the rate across all customers.

### Level 3 — Verified Finding

Operational or other reliable data confirms the pattern.

**Example:**

> Contact-center data confirms that repeat contacts increased during the last quarter.

This is a stronger basis for action.

### Level 4 — Confirmed Root Cause

Investigation provides evidence that a particular process or condition is causing the problem.

**Example:**

> Case review shows that customers are being transferred because the routing rules do not cover a specific request type.

This level requires investigation beyond customer comments.

---

## 4. Questions the Reviewer Should Ask

Before approving an AI finding, ask:

1. What evidence supports this?
2. What evidence is missing?
3. Are we making an assumption?
4. Could there be another explanation?
5. How many customers are actually affected?
6. Is the issue occurring now or only historically?
7. Is the proposed solution addressing the real problem?
8. How would we test the proposed improvement?
9. What metric would tell us whether it worked?
10. Who should own the next step?

---

## 5. Avoiding Common AI Analysis Errors

### Error: Treating One Comment as a Trend

**Problem:** One customer reports a long wait, so the AI concludes that wait times are generally too long.

**Human review:** Check actual wait-time data and additional feedback.

---

### Error: Turning a Customer Statement Into a Verified Fact

**Problem:** A customer says a system was down, and the analysis states that the system experienced an outage.

**Human review:** Check system-status or incident records.

---

### Error: Assuming the Root Cause

**Problem:** Customers report repeated transfers, and the AI concludes that employees are poorly trained.

**Human review:** Investigate routing rules, staffing, procedures, training, and case ownership before assigning a cause.

---

### Error: Recommending a Solution Too Early

**Problem:** Customers report difficulty finding information, and the AI immediately recommends rebuilding the website.

**Human review:** Determine whether navigation, search, content, labeling, or another issue is actually responsible.

---

### Error: Ignoring Positive Feedback

**Problem:** The analysis focuses only on complaints.

**Human review:** Identify successful customer experiences and behaviors that should be preserved or replicated.

---

### Error: Automatically Assigning Blame

**Problem:** A recurring problem is attributed to an employee, team, or department without sufficient evidence.

**Human review:** Examine the process and available evidence before assigning responsibility.

---

## 6. Human Review Decision

After reviewing an AI-generated finding, the reviewer can classify it as:

### Accepted for Investigation

The finding appears useful enough to investigate further.

### Needs More Evidence

The finding may be useful, but additional data is required.

### Revised

The general idea is useful, but the AI's interpretation needs to be changed.

### Rejected

The evidence does not support the finding or it is not relevant.

### Escalate

The issue may involve significant customer, legal, regulatory, security, safety, financial, or reputational concerns and requires the appropriate human authority.

## 7. Recommended Review Record

A human reviewer should be able to document the decision.

| Field | Example |
|---|---|
| AI Finding | Repeat contacts may be increasing |
| Evidence Reviewed | Customer feedback + contact data |
| Human Assessment | Needs more evidence |
| Evidence Missing | Monthly repeat-contact rate |
| Decision | Investigate |
| Owner | Customer Service Manager |
| Next Step | Review 50 repeat-contact cases |
| Measure | Repeat-contact rate |
| Review Date | TBD |

## 8. Improvement Testing

Whenever practical, test an improvement before implementing it broadly.

A basic test should identify:

- The problem being addressed
- The proposed change
- The customer group affected
- The owner
- The timeframe
- The success measure
- Possible unintended effects
- The criteria for continuing, changing, or stopping the test

### Example

**Problem:** Customers may be contacting support repeatedly for status updates.

**Possible test:** Provide clearer status information for a defined group of cases.

**Measure:** Number of status-check contacts before and after the change.

**Human decision:** Review results before expanding the change.

---

## 9. Sensitive or High-Risk Issues

AI-generated findings involving potentially serious matters should receive additional human review.

Examples include:

- Safety concerns
- Security incidents
- Privacy concerns
- Legal or regulatory matters
- Financial harm
- Discrimination or harassment allegations
- Threats
- Serious customer escalation
- Potential fraud

The AI should help organize and surface the information. It should not independently determine the appropriate legal, disciplinary, regulatory, or safety response.

---

## 10. Data Quality Review

Human reviewers should also consider whether the underlying feedback is representative.

Check for:

- Duplicate feedback
- Missing dates
- Missing channels
- Duplicate customers
- Incomplete comments
- Biased samples
- Changes in how feedback was collected
- Outdated feedback
- Extremely unusual cases

A pattern can appear stronger or weaker depending on how the data was collected.

---

## 11. Final Human Review Checklist

Before an improvement recommendation moves forward:

- [ ] The original feedback was reviewed.
- [ ] The important evidence was verified.
- [ ] Patterns were distinguished from isolated incidents.
- [ ] Assumptions were identified.
- [ ] Possible root causes were investigated.
- [ ] Customer impact was considered.
- [ ] Business/process impact was considered.
- [ ] Alternative explanations were considered.
- [ ] Positive experiences were considered.
- [ ] The proposed improvement was reviewed by the appropriate person.
- [ ] A measurement plan exists.
- [ ] Ownership is clear.
- [ ] Any high-risk issue was escalated appropriately.

---

## Final Principle

> **AI can help us see the pattern. Human judgment determines what the pattern means, what evidence is still needed, and what should happen next.**

The goal is not to remove people from customer-experience improvement.

The goal is to help people spend less time sorting through information and more time understanding customers, validating evidence, and making thoughtful improvements.
