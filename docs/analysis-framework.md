# Customer Experience Analysis Framework

## Purpose

This framework defines how the AI Customer Experience Improvement System analyzes customer feedback and turns unstructured information into organized, reviewable insights.

The goal is not simply to summarize what customers said.

The goal is to help identify:

- What customers are experiencing
- What problems appear repeatedly
- What patterns may exist
- What the possible underlying causes are
- How customers may be affected
- What should be investigated
- What improvements could be considered

AI provides structured analysis. A human reviews the findings before any business decision is made.

---

## The Analysis Process

The system follows nine stages:

**1. IDENTIFY THE CUSTOMER ISSUE**

**2. CATEGORIZE THE FEEDBACK**

**3. IDENTIFY RECURRING PATTERNS**

**4. SEPARATE FACTS FROM ASSUMPTIONS**

**5. IDENTIFY POSSIBLE ROOT CAUSES**

**6. DETERMINE CUSTOMER IMPACT**

**7. DETERMINE BUSINESS OR PROCESS IMPACT**

**8. RECOMMEND POSSIBLE IMPROVEMENTS**

**9. FLAG ITEMS FOR HUMAN REVIEW**

---

# 1. Identify the Customer Issue

First, determine what the customer is actually reporting.

The analysis should answer:

- What happened?
- What did the customer expect to happen?
- What actually happened?
- What part of the experience created difficulty?

### Example

Customer feedback:

> "I've called three times and keep getting transferred to different departments."

Identified issue:

**The customer is experiencing repeated transfers when attempting to resolve an issue.**

The system should describe the issue without exaggerating or adding information that the customer did not provide.

---

# 2. Categorize the Feedback

Each feedback item should be assigned one or more useful categories.

Possible categories include:

- Communication
- Wait Time
- Product or Service
- Billing
- Website or Technology
- Customer Support
- Employee Interaction
- Process
- Accessibility
- Information or Instructions
- Service Recovery
- Other

Categories can be expanded when a business has specific needs.

---

# 3. Identify Recurring Patterns

Individual complaints may reveal a larger issue when viewed together.

The system should compare feedback and look for:

- Repeated complaints
- Similar customer experiences
- Common points of confusion
- Repeated delays
- Repeated requests
- Common employee or department references
- Similar problems occurring at different stages of the customer journey

### Important

A single complaint does not automatically establish a recurring pattern.

The system should indicate when there is:

- Strong evidence of a pattern
- Limited evidence
- Insufficient information

---

# 4. Separate Facts From Assumptions

This is one of the most important safeguards in the system.

The analysis should distinguish between:

### Observed Information

What the customer actually reported or what the available data directly shows.

### Interpretation

What the information may suggest.

### Assumption

Something that may be true but has not been verified.

### Example

**Observed:**

Customers report being transferred multiple times.

**Possible interpretation:**

The current routing process may not be directing customers to the correct department.

**Unverified assumption:**

The routing system itself is malfunctioning.

The system should never present the assumption as a confirmed fact.

---

# 5. Identify Possible Root Causes

The system should look beyond the immediate complaint and identify possible contributing causes.

Potential root-cause areas include:

- Training
- Communication
- Technology
- Staffing
- Workflow
- Policies
- Documentation
- Handoffs between departments
- unclear instructions
- System limitations
- Process design

Root causes should be described as **possible causes** unless supporting evidence confirms them.

### Example

Customer complaint:

> "I had to explain my problem to three different people."

Possible contributing causes:

- Incomplete information during handoffs
- Inconsistent documentation
- Lack of ownership of the customer issue
- Departmental communication gaps

These are areas for investigation, not automatically confirmed causes.

---

# 6. Determine Customer Impact

The system should consider how the issue may affect the customer.

Possible impacts include:

- Increased effort
- Frustration
- Longer resolution time
- Confusion
- Loss of trust
- Reduced satisfaction
- Repeated contacts
- Abandoned transactions
- Difficulty completing a task

The system should distinguish between:

**Reported impact** — directly stated by the customer.

**Potential impact** — a reasonable possibility that requires review.

---

# 7. Determine Business or Process Impact

Customer experience problems can also create operational consequences.

Possible business or process impacts include:

- Increased support volume
- Repeated contacts
- Longer handling time
- Employee workload
- Escalations
- Rework
- Lost opportunities
- Process inefficiency
- Increased training needs

These impacts should be presented as possibilities unless the business has data supporting them.

---

# 8. Recommend Possible Improvements

The system should generate practical improvement opportunities based on the available evidence.

Recommendations should be:

- Specific
- Realistic
- Connected to the identified problem
- Easy for a human reviewer to evaluate

### Example

**Problem:**

Customers are repeatedly transferred.

**Possible improvement:**

Review the most common reasons for transfers and determine whether call-routing instructions or department ownership can be clarified.

The system should avoid recommending major changes when the available evidence is limited.

---

# 9. Flag Items for Human Review

Every analysis should identify information that requires human verification.

Examples include:

- Unverified root causes
- Conflicting feedback
- Missing information
- Unclear customer intent
- Possible policy issues
- Possible employee-performance concerns
- Recommendations requiring operational data
- Issues involving privacy or sensitive information

### Human Review Questions

Before taking action, the reviewer should ask:

1. Is the analysis supported by the available information?
2. Are we confusing an assumption with a fact?
3. Is there enough evidence to identify a pattern?
4. Does the proposed root cause need additional investigation?
5. What additional information would help?
6. Is the recommendation practical?
7. Could the proposed change create unintended consequences?

---

# Confidence Levels

The system should use simple confidence language.

### High Confidence

The available information directly supports the finding.

### Moderate Confidence

The finding is supported by multiple indicators but requires some verification.

### Low Confidence

The finding is based on limited information or reasonable possibilities.

### Needs Investigation

There is not enough information to make a reliable determination.

---

# Recommended Analysis Output

Each analyzed feedback item should produce a structured result.

## Customer Issue

What happened?

## Category

What type of issue is this?

## Pattern

Does this appear to be part of a larger recurring issue?

## Evidence

What information supports the finding?

## Possible Root Cause

What could be contributing to the problem?

## Customer Impact

How might the customer be affected?

## Business or Process Impact

How might the organization be affected?

## Recommended Improvement

What could the organization consider doing?

## Confidence

How strong is the available evidence?

## Human Review Needed

What should a person verify before action is taken?

---

# Core Principle

The system should always follow this rule:

> **AI can identify possibilities. People verify reality.**

The purpose of the framework is to help people see patterns and opportunities they may not have time to identify manually.

The final decision remains with the human reviewer.

---

# Quality Checklist

Before an analysis is considered complete, verify:

- [ ] The customer's actual issue has been identified.
- [ ] The feedback has been categorized.
- [ ] Patterns are supported by available evidence.
- [ ] Facts are separated from assumptions.
- [ ] Possible root causes are clearly identified as possibilities when unverified.
- [ ] Customer impact has been considered.
- [ ] Business/process impact has been considered.
- [ ] Recommendations are connected to the evidence.
- [ ] Uncertainty has been identified.
- [ ] Human review requirements are documented.
- [ ] No customer information has been invented.
- [ ] No sensitive information has been unnecessarily included.

---
