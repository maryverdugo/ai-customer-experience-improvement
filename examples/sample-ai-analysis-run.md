# Sample AI Customer Experience Analysis Run

## Purpose

This example demonstrates how the AI Customer Experience Improvement System can take customer feedback, identify patterns, explore possible causes, assess impact, and prepare improvement options for human review.

All information in this example is fictional.

---

## 1. Customer Feedback Input

### Feedback A

> "I had to explain my issue to three different employees before someone could help me."

**Initial AI interpretation:**
- Issue category: Repeated contact / transfers
- Customer impact: Frustration, extra effort, longer resolution time
- Evidence level: Customer-reported
- Verification needed: Review contact history and transfer records

### Feedback B

> "The first person I spoke with was friendly, but they transferred me to another department and I had to explain everything again."

**Initial AI interpretation:**
- Issue category: Transfer / communication
- Customer impact: Repetition and frustration
- Evidence level: Customer-reported
- Verification needed: Review call routing and account notes

### Feedback C

> "I waited a long time before anyone answered my question."

**Initial AI interpretation:**
- Issue category: Wait time
- Customer impact: Time loss and dissatisfaction
- Evidence level: Customer-reported
- Verification needed: Review queue and response-time data

---

## 2. Pattern Detection

### Potential Pattern 1: Customers Repeating Their Situation

Two of the three feedback records describe customers having to repeat information after being transferred.

**Pattern status:** Potential recurring pattern

**Evidence strength:** Moderate

The sample is too small to establish that this is a widespread problem. Additional feedback and operational data should be reviewed.

### Potential Pattern 2: Waiting for Assistance

One customer reported a long wait.

**Pattern status:** Individual issue requiring investigation

One comment alone does not establish a recurring wait-time problem.

---

## 3. Possible Root Causes

The AI should not assume the root cause.

Possible explanations to investigate include:

- Transfers may not include complete customer context.
- Customer notes may not be consistently available to the next employee.
- Routing rules may send customers between departments unnecessarily.
- Employees may lack clear ownership guidelines for certain issues.
- Staffing or queue volume may contribute to longer wait times.

These are hypotheses, not confirmed root causes.

---

## 4. Customer Impact

If repeated transfers are confirmed as a recurring issue, possible customer effects include:

- Increased customer effort
- Frustration
- Longer resolution times
- Reduced confidence in the organization
- Increased likelihood of repeat contact

These impacts should be validated with customer feedback and operational data.

---

## 5. Business Impact to Investigate

Possible business effects include:

- Longer handling time
- Increased repeat contacts
- Higher workload for employees
- Lower customer satisfaction
- Additional transfer volume

The organization should verify these effects using available operational metrics.

---

## 6. Improvement Options

Possible improvements for human review:

### Option A — Improve Transfer Context

Create a standard process for transferring customer information and issue context between departments.

### Option B — Review Routing Rules

Analyze whether certain customer issues are being routed to the wrong team.

### Option C — Clarify Ownership

Define which team or employee should own an issue through resolution.

### Option D — Improve Knowledge Resources

Create or improve internal guidance for common customer issues that currently require multiple contacts.

---

## 7. Human Review Required

Before making a change, a human reviewer should verify:

- Does the transfer problem appear in additional customer feedback?
- Do contact records confirm repeated transfers?
- Which departments are involved?
- Are routing rules contributing to the issue?
- Is the problem caused by process, training, staffing, or another factor?
- Which improvement would address the verified cause?

AI does not make the final decision.

---

## 8. Example Human-Reviewed Action Plan

**Problem:** Customers may be repeating information after transfers.

**Evidence:** Multiple fictional customer comments describe repeated explanations.

**Verification needed:** Review contact records, transfer data, and additional feedback.

**Proposed test:** Pilot a standardized transfer-summary process with one team.

**Success measures:**
- Reduction in repeat explanations
- Reduction in transfer volume
- Reduction in repeat contacts
- Improved customer satisfaction
- Employee feedback on the new process

**Review period:** Evaluate results after an agreed testing period.

---

## 9. Final AI Assessment

The available sample suggests that repeated transfers and customer effort may be areas worth investigating.

The evidence does not establish a confirmed root cause.

The recommended next step is **human verification using additional customer feedback and operational data before implementing a process change.**

> **AI can identify possibilities. People verify reality and decide what to change.**
